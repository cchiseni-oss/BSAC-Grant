# Economic History Research Guide: BSAC Archive Catalogue (Northern Rhodesia)

This guide was compiled from `BSAC Complete Catalogue.xlsx` (954 catalogue entries). It groups the catalogue into economic history themes, suggests research questions each theme can support, and lists the files to consult for each question.

## How to read the references

- **Ref. No.** is the archive reference. The same Ref. No. sometimes appears in more than one collection (for example, `A1/1/1` exists in both the North-Eastern and North-Western Rhodesia series, and `B1/1/1` appears twice). Always use **Ref. No. together with Tag No. and Loc. No.** to find the right folder.
- **Row** is the row number in the Excel catalogue, so you can check any entry against the spreadsheet.
- Dates are as written in the catalogue (two-digit years, e.g. `09` = 1909). Many entries have no year or are marked 'Date cut off'.
- Suggestions are based only on catalogue descriptions. A file's actual content may be richer or thinner than its description.

## Gaps and limitations

- **Few long statistical series.** The catalogue is mostly correspondence. The best numeric sources are the customs reports (rows 396, 502), the 1909-10 district revenue and expenditure reports (rows 269-276), the Luapula census and hut-tax registers (rows 98-100), the revenue estimates (rows 332, 340, 865) and the agricultural statistics (row 910).
- **Missing dates.** Many B1 and HC/1/3 entries have no year, so you will need to date them from their contents.
- **Thin coverage of the Copperbelt boom.** Copper appears only in its early phase (Kansanshi, Bwana Mkubwa, Nkana water rights). The large-scale Copperbelt development after 1924 is outside this collection.
- **Little on African-run trade and markets.** African commerce mostly shows up indirectly, through tax, credit-sale regulation, beer and tobacco excise, and crop reports.
- **Legal series are mostly criminal.** The High Court out-letters (HC3/3, rows 150-193) are almost all murder and death-sentence files, with little economic content. The civil, bankruptcy and debt files (rows 214-228, 257-259) are the economically useful part.
- **Gaps the catalogue itself notes.** Examples: row 3 (gaps 1896-97 and 1898-99), row 16 (letters 82-128 missing) and row 29 (pages 2-16 missing).
